Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions.

What are the entry requirements for the ACCA Qualification?

ACCA Professional Qualification: you need either completed A Levels or a bachelor's degree (university degree; specialisation is not important).

ACCA Foundation Diploma (AFD): to enrol at the Fundamentals level, you need a relevant secondary school education covering five separate subjects, which must include English and mathematics.

What documents do I need to register as an ACCA student?

You are required to submit copies of: official educational documents (completion certificates and transcripts detailing the modules studied), proof of identity (birth certificate, passport, national ID card or driver's licence), and a passport-style photograph.

Where documents have been issued in a language other than English, ACCA requires a photocopy of the original language documents and a copy of an official English translation.

How long does it take to register as an ACCA student?
The online application takes no more than 15 minutes to complete, provided you have your documents ready. You can save a part-complete application and return to it later. You'll receive confirmation by email or letter once your application has been processed — student IDs usually arrive within ten days.
What is the ACCA fee structure?

Initial registration fee: between £45 and £89 depending on your country. Annual subscription fee: £122 payable in January every year while you continue your education. Exemption fees: if you are awarded exemptions, these are charged at the early exam entry rate for that paper.

For a full list of current prices, visit the fees section of the main ACCA website.

Which exam papers do I have to take to complete the qualification?
To qualify as an ACCA member you will need to complete 13 exams (students with prior qualifications may be able to claim exemptions at Fundamentals level), three years' practical work experience, and the Ethics and Professional Skills module.
When do ACCA exams take place?

On-demand CBE: four papers — BT, MA, FA and LW — have no fixed date and can be taken any time in the year. Book with an exam centre near you and pay locally; results are given immediately on submission.

Session-based exams: all other papers run in four exam sessions, in the first week of March, June, September and December. Results are sent by email or SMS after six weeks.

How much does the ACCA qualification cost, and how long does it take?

With a bachelor's degree you need to pass thirteen exams (some may be exempted depending on your university), taking approximately 2.5 to 3 years.

Registration fee: £45–£79 depending on your country. Exam fees: roughly $100–150 per paper — around $1,800 across 13 exams over three years. Tuition fees: depend on where you take your exam preparation.
