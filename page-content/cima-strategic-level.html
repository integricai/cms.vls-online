What is the CIMA Strategic Level?

The Strategic Level is the third and final level of the CIMA Professional Qualification — the exams that separate students from members. The three-pillar structure completes at board level: E3 Strategic Management (Enterprise pillar), P3 Risk Management (Performance pillar) and F3 Financial Strategy (Financial pillar), capped by the Strategic Case Study (SCS).

The focus is the long term: formulating strategy, managing enterprise-wide risk, and making the big financing decisions. E3, P3 and F3 are 90-minute on-demand objective tests with instant provisional results; the SCS is a three-hour written exam based on a pre-seen company, held in four windows a year (February, May, August and November). Pass the SCS and your exams are complete — verify three years of practical experience and you earn ACMA membership and the CGMA designation.

What role do you play in the Strategic Case Study?

In the SCS you're a senior finance adviser to the board. Tasks integrate the three papers at the highest level: evaluating strategic options and stakeholder impacts from E3, designing risk responses from P3, and advising on financing, dividend policy and business valuation from F3. The examiners reward commercial judgement and clear board-level communication — exactly what our marked mocks are graded on.

Questions, answered

CIMA Strategic Level FAQs.

What is the CIMA Strategic Level?
It's the final level of the CIMA Professional Qualification: three objective tests — E3 (Strategic Management), P3 (Risk Management) and F3 (Financial Strategy) — followed by the Strategic Case Study. Passing the SCS completes the exam component of CIMA.
What's the exam format?
E3, P3 and F3 are 90-minute computer-based objective tests, on demand year-round with provisional results immediately. The Strategic Case Study is a 3-hour written exam based on pre-seen material, held in four windows a year: February, May, August and November.
What role do I play in the SCS?
You act as a senior finance adviser to the board of the pre-seen company. Tasks integrate all three pillars — strategic options from E3, risk responses from P3, and financing, valuation and dividend decisions from F3 — communicated in clear, board-ready language.
In what order should I take E3, P3 and F3?
Any order works. A popular route is E3 first, then P3, taking F3 last and closest to your SCS window — its financing and valuation content features heavily in board-level case study tasks. We'll plan the right order for you in a free consultation.
What happens after I pass the SCS?
Your exams are done. To become an ACMA member and hold the CGMA designation, you submit three years of relevant practical experience through CIMA's PER process for verification. Many students bank this experience while studying, so membership can follow within weeks of the final exam.
How long does the Strategic Level take?
Most working students complete the three objective tests and the case study in nine to fifteen months. The on-demand OTs let you set your own pace before targeting the SCS window that fits.
Is the Strategic Case Study the hardest CIMA exam?
It's the most senior in tone, but well-prepared candidates often find it the most predictable: the pre-seen tells you the company, and disciplined preparation narrows the likely task areas. Students who invest in pre-seen analysis and timed mock practice — the core of our SCS course — consistently pass first time.
