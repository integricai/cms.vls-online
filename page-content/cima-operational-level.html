What is the CIMA Operational Level?

The Operational Level is the first level of the CIMA Professional Qualification — the stage after the Certificate in Business Accounting (or your degree exemptions). It follows CIMA's three-pillar structure: E1 Managing Finance in a Digital World (Enterprise pillar), P1 Management Accounting (Performance pillar) and F1 Financial Reporting (Financial pillar), capped by the Operational Case Study (OCS).

E1, P1 and F1 are 90-minute objective tests, available on demand year-round with provisional results on the spot. Once you've passed all three, you sit the OCS — a three-hour written exam based on a pre-seen fictional company, held in four windows each year (February, May, August and November). Pass it and you earn the CIMA Diploma in Management Accounting (CIMA Dip MA).

What does the Operational Level focus on?

The theme is the short term: implementing decisions, managing costs, preparing financial statements and working with technology in the finance function. In the case study you play a finance officer, applying everything from the three papers to a realistic business scenario — the format CIMA is famous for, and the reason employers rate the qualification so highly.

Questions, answered

CIMA Operational Level FAQs.

What is the CIMA Operational Level?
It's the first level of the CIMA Professional Qualification: three objective tests — E1 (Managing Finance in a Digital World), P1 (Management Accounting) and F1 (Financial Reporting) — followed by the Operational Case Study. Completing it earns you the CIMA Diploma in Management Accounting.
What's the exam format?
E1, P1 and F1 are 90-minute computer-based objective tests, on demand year-round, with provisional results immediately. The Operational Case Study is a 3-hour written exam based on pre-seen material, held in four windows a year: February, May, August and November.
Do I need to pass all three OTs before the case study?
Yes. The OCS integrates knowledge from all three pillars around one simulated company, so you must pass (or be exempt from) E1, P1 and F1 before sitting it.
In what order should I take E1, P1 and F1?
Any order works. Many students start with E1 as the most narrative paper, then F1, saving P1 — usually rated the hardest at this level — for when momentum is built. We'll plan the right order for your background in a free consultation.
What is the pre-seen, and how do you help with it?
About seven weeks before each case study window, CIMA publishes information about the fictional company the exam is based on. Our OCS course includes a full pre-seen breakdown — business model, industry analysis, likely task areas — plus answer-planning technique and a marked mock.
How long does the Operational Level take?
Most working students complete the three objective tests and the case study in nine to fifteen months. Because the OTs are on demand, you set the pace, then target the case study window that fits.
What comes after the Operational Level?
The Management Level — E2, P2, F2 and the Management Case Study — followed by the Strategic Level. Complete all three levels plus three years of practical experience and you earn ACMA membership and the CGMA designation.
