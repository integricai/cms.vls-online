What is the CIMA Management Level?

The Management Level is the second level of the CIMA Professional Qualification, continuing the three-pillar structure a step deeper: E2 Managing Performance (Enterprise pillar), P2 Advanced Management Accounting (Performance pillar) and F2 Advanced Financial Reporting (Financial pillar), capped by the Management Case Study (MCS).

The theme shifts from the short term to the medium term — from implementing decisions to managing people, projects and performance. E2, P2 and F2 are 90-minute on-demand objective tests with instant provisional results; the MCS is a three-hour written exam based on a pre-seen company, held in four windows a year (February, May, August and November). Pass it and you earn the CIMA Advanced Diploma in Management Accounting (CIMA Adv Dip MA).

What role do you play in the Management Case Study?

In the MCS you're the finance manager, advising senior management of the pre-seen business. Tasks weave the three papers together: evaluating projects and pricing decisions from P2, group reporting and financial analysis from F2, and the people, change and relationship management themes of E2. It's the closest CIMA gets to a day in a real finance manager's job — and it rewards candidates who know the pre-seen company inside out.

Questions, answered

CIMA Management Level FAQs.

What is the CIMA Management Level?
It's the second level of the CIMA Professional Qualification: three objective tests — E2 (Managing Performance), P2 (Advanced Management Accounting) and F2 (Advanced Financial Reporting) — followed by the Management Case Study. Completing it earns you the CIMA Advanced Diploma in Management Accounting.
What's the exam format?
E2, P2 and F2 are 90-minute computer-based objective tests, on demand year-round with provisional results immediately. The Management Case Study is a 3-hour written exam based on pre-seen material, held in four windows a year: February, May, August and November.
What role do I play in the MCS?
You act as a finance manager advising senior management of the pre-seen company. Tasks integrate all three pillars — expect project appraisal and pricing from P2, group reporting from F2, and the people, change and relationship themes of E2, all applied to the scenario.
In what order should I take E2, P2 and F2?
Any order works. A common route is E2 first (most narrative), then F2, saving P2 — generally rated the toughest — for last with the most preparation time. We'll plan the right order for your background in a free consultation.
How long does the Management Level take?
Most working students complete the three objective tests and the case study in nine to fifteen months. The on-demand OTs let you set your own pace before targeting the MCS window that fits.
Is the Management Level harder than Operational?
Yes, noticeably — P2 and F2 are more technical than their Operational counterparts, and the MCS expects more commercial judgement in a more senior role. Consistent question practice and thorough pre-seen preparation are what carry students through, and both are core to our courses.
What comes after the Management Level?
The Strategic Level — E3, P3, F3 and the Strategic Case Study. Complete it, plus three years of verified practical experience, and you earn ACMA membership and the CGMA designation.
